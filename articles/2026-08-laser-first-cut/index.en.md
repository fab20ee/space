The laser cuts exactly what is drawn in the file — and mistakes in the file cost more than
mistakes at the machine. Five rules that save material on the first evening.

## Vectors only

The machine understands outlines, not pictures. A raster image is fine for engraving only;
everything that should be cut is drawn with lines and curves — in Inkscape, Illustrator, Fusion
or any vector editor. Export to SVG or DXF, and convert text to curves before exporting.

## Hairline strokes

The machine software cuts along the centre of a line and does not care about its width — but
fills and thick outlines may be taken for engraving areas. Make every cutting outline a hairline
with no fill.

## Colour is the operation

Red — cut, blue — line engraving, black — raster engraving: with colour you tell the machine
what to do with each outline. Settle on a palette and stick to it; the order of operations is set
there too — engrave first, then inner cut-outs, the outer outline last, so the part does not move
too early.

## Check the scale

Units in the file must be millimetres, not pixels. One unticked box on export and the part comes
out 3.78 times larger. Draw a 10 × 10 mm square next to your design and measure it in the machine
software.

## A test cut on scrap

Power and speed for your material are in the table next to the machine, but material varies. A
small square on scrap from the same sheet is a minute of time and protection from a ruined
blank. Test sheets are next to the machine and included in the plan.
