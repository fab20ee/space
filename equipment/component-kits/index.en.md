The shelf of kits you take a specific value from when you need it right now. Separate from the
[consumables boxes](/en/equipment/electronics-components-and-consumables): those hold small parts
mixed together, this holds them sorted by value.

## The SMD books

The main thing on the shelf. A binder where every page is a row of strips of resistors or
capacitors, each strip labelled with its value and tolerance: `R0805 4.7K ±5%` and so on.

There are several books, in different sizes. That matters: **0603 and 0805 are not
interchangeable**. If your board is laid out for 0805, an 0603 part will sit badly on it and a
1206 will not fit at all. Check which book you are taking from.

Take the part out of the strip carefully and put the strip back on its ring. A book with the
strips out of order is useless: there is no way to identify an SMD resistor's value by eye.

## Through-hole parts

Resistors on paper tape, labelled by hand with their values, plus diodes, transistors and the rest
loose. Through-hole is easier on a breadboard and for anything built point-to-point.

## Electrolytics and LEDs

Two ready kits in compartment boxes: electrolytic capacitors in several dozen values from
fractions of a microfarad to thousands, and LEDs in 3 and 5 mm in various colours.

Electrolytics have a polarity and a working voltage, both printed on the can. A 6.3 V capacitor in
a 12 V circuit swells up — sometimes at once, sometimes a week later.

## How to use

1. Take what you need for the job in front of you.
2. **Put the strip or the part back in the same place.** That is the one rule here that really
   matters.
3. If a value has run out or a compartment is empty, tell us and we restock.
4. If you are unsure of a value, check it on the [UT612 LCR
   meter](/en/equipment/lcr-meter-uni-t-ut612) or the bench multimeter.

Components are included in your access within reason.

## Access

The shelf is in the hackspace room, so a plan that includes hackspace access is required. No
induction is needed.
