A compact, fully enclosed CoreXY printer. The closed chamber is the point: it keeps the air
around the part warm, so ABS and ASA come out without warping or cracking — something the open
printers in the room cannot promise. The build volume is small, but the machine is quick and
the parts are precise.

Use it for functional parts that need heat resistance or toughness: gears, clips, snap-fit
housings, brackets for anything that lives near a motor or in a car.

## What you can make

- ABS and ASA parts that must survive heat, sun or repeated flexing
- Small precise mechanisms: gears, hinges, snap fits
- Housings for sensors and small electronics
- Quick prototypes while a bigger printer is busy

## Materials

**Works well:** ABS and ASA (with the doors closed), PLA and PETG (with the top cover and front
door open — see below), TPU.

**Ask first:** abrasive filaments (carbon or glass filled) and anything that needs more than
280 °C at the nozzle.

## Safety

- The nozzle runs at up to 280 °C and the bed and chamber get hot: let the machine cool before
  reaching inside.
- ABS and ASA give off fumes — keep the doors closed while printing them and ventilate the room
  afterwards.
- Do not leave a first layer unattended.
- Remove parts with the print sheet off the bed, not with a knife inside the machine.

## How to use

1. Slice the model in QIDI Slicer or OrcaSlicer with the profile for the loaded filament.
2. For PLA and TPU open the top cover and the front door before starting — otherwise the chamber
   gets too warm, the filament softens and the nozzle clogs. For ABS and ASA keep them closed.
3. Check that the filament loaded matches your profile.
4. Start the print, stay for the first layer, and check the part after the first few
   millimetres.
5. When done, let the chamber cool, take the part off, clean the plate, and put leftover
   filament back in its bag with the desiccant.

## Access

The printer is in the hackspace room, so a plan that includes hackspace access is required.
No induction is needed, but ask someone to show you the filament change the first time.
