A digital microscope is a camera on a stand with a screen: you look at the display, not into an
eyepiece, so your hands and the soldering iron have room under the lens and you can work for an
hour without a stiff neck. At this magnification a 0402 resistor fills a good part of the screen
and the pins of a 0.5 mm pitch chip are clearly separate.

It is the companion to the soldering stations: inspect joints, spot bridges and cold solder,
read the marking on a part too small for the naked eye, and place SMD components by hand.

## What it is good for

- Inspecting solder joints after hot air or reflow work
- Soldering fine-pitch parts under magnification
- Reading component markings and PCB reference designators
- Looking for cracked traces, corrosion or lifted pads
- Photographing a board for a repair log or a question in a forum

## Safety

- The microscope itself is harmless, but the soldering iron under it is not — keep the lens well
  above the work so you do not touch it with the hot tip.
- Do not touch the lens. Dust comes off with a blower; fingerprints do not.
- Do not move the stand while the iron is under it.

## How to use

1. Switch on with the power button; the image appears on the screen.
2. Put the board on the base and lower the camera until the picture is roughly sharp, then
   fine-focus with the ring on the lens.
3. Switch the gooseneck lamps on and aim them from two sides to kill shadows and glare.
4. Use the up/down buttons for digital zoom, the camera button for a photo, M for the menu.
5. When done, raise the camera, switch off, and leave the stand clear.

## Access

The microscope is on the electronics bench in the hackspace room, so a plan that includes
hackspace access is required. No induction is needed.
