Tools like this are usually called a "Dremel", after the brand that invented them. The generic
name is a rotary tool: a motor with a collet on the end that spins a small bit very fast.

It does the jobs a full-size tool is too crude for: cutting a protruding lead flush, tidying the
edge of a printed part, opening out a hole in an enclosure, removing a burr, polishing a contact,
drilling a half-millimetre hole.

It is cordless, so it goes where a cable will not and you do not unwind an extension lead for
three seconds of work.

## The bits

The consumables are sorted into boxes, and there are more of them than you would think:

- **Cut-off discs** — thin reinforced ones and diamond ones. They cut metal, plastic and circuit
  boards.
- **Grinding stones and drums** — removing material and levelling an edge.
- **Burrs** — cutting a slot, opening out a hole, engraving.
- **Brushes and polishing wheels** with compound — cleaning contacts and finishing a surface.
- **Micro drills** — from fractions of a millimetre, for boards and small enclosures.

A bit goes into a collet **of the matching size**. Clamping a thin shank in a large collet means
runout, a bit torn loose and, with some probability, a fragment in your face.

## Safety

The tool is small, but its speed is very high, and that changes everything.

- **Eye protection is mandatory.** A cut-off disc that snags shatters, and the pieces fly in the
  plane of the disc — which is to say, at your face.
- **Clamp the work** rather than holding it. The tool will jerk the work, not the other way round.
- Do not push. A rotary tool cuts with speed, not force; pressure breaks bits and heats the
  material.
- Hair, sleeves and cords stay away from the spinning end.
- Plastic and circuit boards smoke when cut. Work under the [fume
  extractor](/en/equipment/fume-extractor-eleshop-ele-fu1) or with ventilation.
- Change bits only with the tool switched off.

## How to use

1. Choose a bit for the job and a collet for the bit's shank.
2. Tighten the collet with the key and check at low speed that the bit does not wobble.
3. Start slow and go faster if you need to. High speed melts plastic.
4. Guide the tool without pressing, in short passes.
5. When finished, take the bit off, return it to its compartment and the tool to its place.
