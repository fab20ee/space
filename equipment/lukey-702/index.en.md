A simple, proven soldering station: a 50 W iron and a hot air gun with a diaphragm pump, each
with its own temperature readout. It does the same everyday jobs as the ATTEN station — the
Lukey has fewer features, but it is a second station, so two people can solder at once, or one
person can keep the iron in one hand and the hot air in the other.

## What it is good for

- Through-hole soldering and cable work
- SMD rework with hot air: removing and placing small chips
- Heat-shrink tubing and shrinking connector boots
- Teaching someone to solder without occupying the main station

## Safety

- Tip and nozzle exceed 400 °C — do not touch anything metal on the tools until it has cooled.
- Put the iron back in its stand every time. Never leave it lying on the bench.
- The hot air gun keeps blowing for a while after you switch it off — that is the cool-down, do
  not unplug it mid-cycle.
- Keep plastic parts, LCDs and wiring away from the hot air stream, or shield them.
- Use the fume extractor or open a window; wash your hands afterwards.

## How to use

1. Switch on the tool you need with its own power button; the two halves work independently.
2. Set the iron to about 300–350 °C for lead-free solder, and the hot air to around 300 °C with
   a moderate airflow to begin with.
3. Tin the tip before the first joint and wipe it on the damp sponge or brass cleaner between
   joints.
4. When done, clean and tin the tip, switch both tools off, and let the hot air gun finish
   cooling down.
5. Put tips, nozzles and tweezers back where they came from.

## Access

The station is on the electronics bench in the hackspace room, so a plan that includes hackspace
access is required. No induction is needed.
