An ultrasonic bath cleans by making millions of tiny bubbles collapse against the surface of
whatever is submerged. It reaches into places a brush never will: under chips, inside a clogged
nozzle, between the teeth of a gear. Ten minutes in warm water with a drop of detergent takes
flux residue off a freshly soldered board and leaves it looking new.

## What it is good for

- Washing flux off circuit boards after soldering or rework
- Clearing clogged 3D printer nozzles and hot ends
- Cleaning small mechanical parts, screws, bearings and tools
- Glasses, watch straps, jewellery — with the right fluid

## Safety

- Never run the bath empty or with the liquid below the minimum mark: it destroys the
  transducer.
- Unplug before filling and draining. Water and mains do not mix.
- Do not put fingers in the bath while it is running — it is unpleasant and not good for you.
- No flammable solvents (acetone, petrol, IPA in quantity). Water with a little detergent, or
  a cleaning solution made for ultrasonic baths.
- Boards with batteries, unsealed switches, speakers, displays or paper labels do not go in.
  Ask if you are not sure about a component.
- The liquid gets hot with the heater on; take parts out with tweezers.

## How to use

1. Fill with water to between the min and max marks, add a little detergent or cleaning
   solution.
2. Put the parts in the basket — not directly on the bottom of the tank.
3. Set the temperature if you want it warm (40–50 °C is enough for flux) and the timer, usually
   5–10 minutes.
4. Close the lid, start, and wait. Repeat with fresh water for a rinse if the fluid was dirty.
5. Dry the parts thoroughly — compressed air or a warm place — before applying power to a
   board.
6. When done, unplug, empty the tank, wipe it dry and put the lid back.

## Access

The cleaner is in the hackspace room, so a plan that includes hackspace access is required.
No induction is needed.
