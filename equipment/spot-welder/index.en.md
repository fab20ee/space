You do not solder batteries. More precisely, the people who do are the ones who later wonder why
the pack runs hot and loses capacity: soldering means prolonged heating, and an 18650 does not
take that well — both its internal resistance and the safety mechanisms inside suffer.

The right way is to weld nickel strip on: a short pulse of current melts metal at two points in
milliseconds, and the cell itself has no time to heat up.

The welder runs off its own battery, so there is no mains cable anywhere near the cells. The unit
has no name: there is no model and no serial number on it, so everything it does is described
here.

## What is adjustable

Three parameters on the screen:

- **The pre-pulse** — a short pulse before the main one. It breaks through the oxide and presses
  the strip onto the cell, so that the main pulse goes through a proper contact rather than an air
  gap.
- **The main pulse** — the one that actually makes the weld. The longer it is, the stronger the
  spot and the more heat goes into the cell.
- **The delay** between them.

In automatic mode the welder fires by itself as soon as both electrodes touch the strip. There is
no separate trigger, and that is one reason to be careful: contact is already a weld.

## Practise first

**Do not start on real cells.** There is a stock of nickel strip in the container — take a piece
and weld it to something you do not need, an offcut of the same strip, a dead cell.

You need a feel for two things: how the parameters change the spot, and what a good weld looks
like. A weak spot peels off by hand and leaves the strip smooth. A strong one tears the strip but
does not leave the cell — pulling it off leaves a hole in the strip and a trace of metal on the
can. That is what you tune the pulse length for.

## How to use

1. Take the welder and the strip from the container.
2. Practise on offcuts until the spot comes out consistent.
3. Cut the strip to length, lay it on the cell, press the electrodes down.
4. Place the electrodes **close together but not touching**: too close and the current goes along
   the strip instead of through the cell, too far apart and the spots come out uneven.
5. Make two spots per contact, not one.
6. Check by hand: the strip should not lift.
7. Put the welder and the leftover strip back in the container.

## Care with lithium

An 18650 is not a toy. A 2–3 Ah cell can deliver tens of amps into a short circuit, and that ends
exactly one way: a fire that water does not put out.

- Watch that an electrode, the strip or a tool does not bridge plus and minus. In a pack that is
  especially easy: the strip is long and the next cell is right there.
- Do not weld to the side of a cell's can — ends only.
- Do not weld damaged, swollen or suspiciously warm cells.
- Do not leave a half-built pack with bare strips sticking out.
- Work somewhere you could throw a cell if something goes wrong.

If you are unsure, ask us before welding, not after.
