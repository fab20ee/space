The shelf where wire turns into a finished harness. It holds the wire itself, the tools that
prepare it, and the things it ends in.

## Crimping rather than soldering

A wire soldered into a connector is a joint that breaks where it bends: the solder stiffens the
strand exactly where it needs to flex. A crimped contact grips the wire mechanically and leaves
its flexibility alone.

So anything that plugs and unplugs gets a connector with crimp contacts, not wires soldered
straight on.

## The tools

- **Crimpers** of several types. They are not interchangeable: each has a jaw profile for its own
  kind of contact. A crimper for insulated ferrules will ruin a connector contact, and the other
  way round.
- **The stripper** removes insulation without nicking the strands. Side cutters do the same job
  worse and differently every time.
- **The ratchet** in some of the crimpers will not let the jaws open until the crimp is finished.
  That is not a fault, it is protection against a half-made crimp.

## How to crimp properly

1. Strip the insulation to exactly the length of the contact area — no more.
2. Put the wire into the contact so that **the front wings grip the copper and the rear wings grip
   the insulation**. This is the key part: if both sets grip only copper, the wire breaks off at
   the first bend.
3. Crimp with the right profile, until it clicks.
4. **Test it by pulling on the wire.** A good crimp does not come out by hand.
5. Push the contact into the housing until the latch clicks.

## Heat shrink

Assortments of tubing in various diameters. Choose a diameter that slides on freely before
shrinking and grips firmly after. Heat it with the hot-air gun on the soldering station rather
than a lighter: an open flame leaves soot and burns through.

Put the tubing on **before** you join the wires. That is obvious right up until the time you
forget.

## How to use

1. Choose wire by cross-section for the current, not by which colour looks nicer.
2. Put the tools back on the shelf rather than in your own drawer — crimpers wander off fastest.
3. Take contacts and housings from their compartments and return the leftovers to the same ones.
4. If a contact kit runs out, tell us.
