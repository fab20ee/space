To lift a 0603 resistor with a soldering iron you have to heat one end, then the other, and catch
the moment when both are molten at once. Hot tweezers heat both terminals together: squeeze, wait
two seconds, lift the part off.

This is the tool for any board rework that involves taking small things off: resistors,
capacitors, diodes, LEDs, small transistors — anything with two or three legs.

The HT140 does a second job as well: with C210 tips it works as an ordinary fine soldering iron.
That is the "2-in-1" — what changes is the cartridge, not the instrument. The cartridges — C120
tweezer pairs and C210 iron tips — live either in the container with the tool or in the big
consumables box.

## Power

It runs from USB-C PD, from QC, or from an ordinary supply with a barrel jack, 5 to 28 V. How fast
it heats depends on that, and it depends a lot: from a 65 W source it reaches 500 °C in five
seconds, from a 140 W one in three.

Here it runs off one of the shared 65 W USB-C PD bricks: those are plugged into sockets around the
hackspace and kept in the common box of power supplies. With one of those, 500 °C takes those five
seconds.

The screen always shows how many volts it got. If the heating feels sluggish, look at that number
first.

## What it is good for

- Lifting a two-terminal SMD part without cooking its neighbours
- Moving a part: take it off, clean the pads, put it back
- Removing things that are glued as well as soldered — tweezers are better at pulling
- Soldering fine joints with a C210 tip, when tweezers are not what you need
- Working under a microscope: at 50 g it does not drag your hand down

## How to use

1. Plug the power in and let it come up to temperature.
2. Set the temperature. For lifting small SMD parts 300–330 °C is usually enough; massive parts
   and lead-free solder want more.
3. Close the jaws on the part's terminals and let the solder melt on both sides — that is seconds,
   not fractions of a second.
4. Lift the part straight up, without scraping the neighbouring pads.
5. Put the instrument in its stand. The jaws stay hot longer than you think.

## What not to do

- Do not lever against solder that has not melted: you will tear a pad off the board before the
  part comes free. If it does not move, it is not molten yet.
- Do not use tweezers on multi-leg packages. For QFP and BGA there is the [IR6500 rework
  station](/en/equipment/bga-rework-station-ir6500); tweezers would simply rip the legs off.
- Do not change the cartridge while it is hot.
- Do not put it down on the bench until it has cooled.
