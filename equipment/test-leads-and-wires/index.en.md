The container holding whatever connects an instrument to the thing you are measuring. It is a box
of its own because leads are the first thing to spread across a workshop and the first thing
missing when you need one.

This is where the cords for the [power supplies](/en/equipment/power-supply-owon-spe3102) come
from, the crocodile clips for the [electronic load](/en/equipment/electronic-load-150w), and spare
probes for the multimeters when the supplied ones are not enough or the wrong length.

## What is here

- **Banana-to-banana cords** in various colours and lengths. The colour is not decoration: red and
  black for plus and minus saves you one destroyed instrument a year.
- **Crocodile clips** — loose, and already fitted to leads.
- **Probes**, ordinary, with a sharp tip.
- **Bags of connectors**: banana plugs, ferrules, adapters — for anyone making up a lead for a
  particular job.

## Worth remembering

- **Leads have a voltage and a current rating.** A thin probe meant for a multimeter is no good
  for five amps out of a power supply: it heats up, and its insulation is not for mains.
- **Check a lead for continuity if the readings look strange.** A broken strand inside
  intact-looking insulation is the most common cause of "the instrument is lying".
- **Do not cut up leads from this box** for your own projects. There is hook-up wire for that in
  the [component boxes](/en/equipment/electronics-components-and-consumables).

## How to use

1. Take what fits the connector and the current, not what is lying on top.
2. Coil a lead before putting it back. A tangle at the bottom of the box is the next person
   spending five minutes and then taking a different one.
3. Do not quietly return a damaged lead: set it aside and tell us.

## Access

The container is in the hackspace room, so a plan that includes hackspace access is required. No
induction is needed.
