An APS-C camera with a 24 megapixel sensor and two lenses for different jobs.

You want it where a phone runs out: shooting a project for a portfolio, photographing a board so
the markings are legible, covering an event, taking decent product photos of something you intend
to sell.

## The two lenses

- **18–55 mm f/3.5–5.6** — the general-purpose zoom. From a wide shot of a room to a portrait
  without changing lenses. It is not fast, so in a dim room it runs out of light.
- **50 mm f/1.8** — a prime, no zoom, but four times the light. Indoors without flash, with a
  blurred background, for portraits and product shots — this is the one. It makes you walk instead
  of turning a ring, which is not a drawback.

Sony puts the stabilisation in the body, so it works with both lenses — noticeable at the long end
handheld.

## What it is good for

- Shooting your project for the [Wall of Fame](/en/projects) or for an application
- Product photography: an object, a board, a part close up
- Events in the hackspace and the coworking room
- Portraits in poor light — with the 50 mm
- Full HD video

## How to use

1. Take the camera and the lens you want out of the bag. Change lenses **with the camera off**,
   quickly, and facing down so that less dust lands on the sensor.
2. Check the battery and that a memory card is in.
3. Shoot. If you are unsure of the settings, mode `A` (aperture priority) covers most jobs: you
   choose the depth of field and the camera works out the rest.
4. Return the camera, both lenses and the caps to the bag. **The caps are not optional**: an open
   mount and a bare rear element collect dust immediately.

## What not to do

- Do not touch the glass or the sensor with your fingers. A mark on the front element comes off
  with an optics wipe; a sensor is cleaned separately and not on a kitchen table.
- Do not change lenses in wind, in dust, or over a bench covered in swarf.
- Do not carry the camera by the lens — the mount is not built for it.
